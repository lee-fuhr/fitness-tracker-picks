Fitness Tracker questions answered

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

Fitness trackers vary widely in accuracy, features, and price, so the best choice depends on your specific goals and lifestyle. Focus on core sensors (heart rate, step counting), comfort, and battery life before extras like GPS or music storage. Ignore marketing hype, read owner reviews and expert roundups to find what truly works for you.

What does a fitness tracker actually measure?

Most fitness trackers use an accelerometer to count steps and estimate distance, paired with an optical heart rate sensor that shines light through your skin to detect blood volume changes. Many also track sleep by monitoring movement and heart rate throughout the night, then break that down into light, deep, and REM stages. Calories burned are calculated from your heart rate, age, weight, and activity type. These are estimates, not lab-grade measurements, but they are useful for spotting trends over days and weeks. Higher-end models may add SpO2 (blood oxygen), skin temperature, or stress readings, but those features are often less consistent in accuracy. The key takeaway: the core sensors, step count and heart rate, are the ones you will use daily. Extras are nice but should not drive your decision unless you have a specific need, like sleep apnea monitoring.

How accurate are fitness tracker heart rate and steps?

Step counting is generally very reliable across mainstream brands like Fitbit, Garmin, and Apple. You might see small variations compared to a manual tally, but for everyday use the error is small enough to trust for trends. Heart rate accuracy is a different story. Optical sensors struggle with rapid changes in intensity, tight grip on the wrist, or dark skin tones. During steady-state walking or jogging they are fine, but during interval training or lifting weights you may see lag or missed beats. Chest straps are far more accurate for serious athletes, but most casual users find wrist-based HR acceptable. The honest take: use the heart rate data as a general guide for zone training, not as a medical device. For step counts, pick any reputable brand and you will be fine.

Should I get a tracker with built-in GPS?

Built-in GPS lets you map runs and bike rides without carrying your phone, which is a big plus for runners who want light wrists and accurate pace data. However, it draws heavily on the battery, expect to charge every few days if you use GPS regularly. If you exercise mostly indoors (treadmill, gym) or always have your phone with you outside, connected GPS (using your phone’s GPS) is a perfectly good alternative. Many trackers offer this as a battery-saving option. Our advice: only pay extra for onboard GPS if you run or cycle outdoors multiple times a week and dislike bringing your phone. For everyone else, connected GPS saves money and keeps battery life longer.

How important is water resistance?

Almost all fitness trackers are at least splash-proof and can handle sweat, rain, and hand washing. The real question is whether you want to swim with it. True swim tracking requires a water resistance rating of 5 ATM (50 meters) or better. Brands like Garmin, Fitbit, and Apple include swim modes in some models. Even with a high rating, not all trackers log swimming strokes accurately, optical HR is often unreliable when moving through water. If swimming is your main activity, look for a tracker specifically tested for pool use, such as a Garmin Swim series or a dedicated swim watch. A quick word: hot showers and saunas can damage water seals even on high-rated trackers. Rinse the device with fresh water after salt or chlorinated water, and dry it before charging.

What about battery life and charging?

Battery life is one of the biggest trade-offs in fitness trackers. Simple bands that only track steps and sleep can last a week or more between charges. Full-color smartwatch-style trackers with always-on displays and GPS tend to need charging every one to three days. If you dislike remembering to charge another device, lean toward a minimalist tracker with a long battery (around 7–14 days). If you want a rich display and smart notifications, you will have to accept more frequent charging, usually at night while you sleep, unless you also track sleep, in which case you need a short charge during the day. Most mid-range trackers use a proprietary charging cable that clicks onto the back. Magnetic chargers are convenient, but clip-on styles can feel fiddly. Check owner reviews for common complaints about charger durability.

Which fitness tracker brand is best for me?

The best brand depends on your phone and your activity preferences. Fitbit has the widest compatibility (both iPhone and Android) and is known for a user-friendly app and strong community challenges. Garmin is the go-to for serious runners, cyclists, and hikers, with detailed performance metrics and offline maps on higher-end models. Apple Watch works only with iPhones and offers deep health integrations, like ECG and fall detection. Other worthy options: Whoop uses a subscription model and focuses on recovery and strain without a screen; Xiaomi and Amazfit offer budget-friendly trackers with decent accuracy; Samsung wears well with Android phones but has a limited ecosystem. Our straightforward rule: if you have an iPhone and want smartwatch features, get an Apple Watch. If you want a focus on fitness without a subscription, go with Fitbit or Garmin based on your activity level. If cost is a priority, explore Amazfit or Xiaomi, just know the app experience may be less polished.

Frequently asked questions

Can I wear my fitness tracker in the shower?

Most basic fitness trackers are splash-proof and fine for showering, but you should check the water resistance rating. Models rated IP68 or 5 ATM are safest for full immersion.

Does a fitness tracker track sleep accurately?

Sleep tracking is based on movement and heart rate; it can estimate light, deep, and REM sleep but is not as accurate as medical equipment. Useful for seeing patterns over time.

Do I need a subscription to use a fitness tracker?

Most trackers work fully without a subscription. Some brands, like Whoop and Fitbit Premium, offer deeper analysis with a paid plan, but basic features are free.

Will a fitness tracker help me lose weight?

It can be a helpful tool by increasing awareness of activity and calories burned, but it should complement a balanced diet and exercise, not replace them.

Can I use a fitness tracker without a smartphone?

Many trackers require a smartphone for setup and syncing, but some basic models have a mini display that shows time and steps without a phone. However, you’ll miss most features.

How do I clean my fitness tracker?

Wipe the band and sensor with a damp cloth, avoid harsh chemicals. Remove the band if possible. Moisture under the strap can cause skin irritation; dry it thoroughly.

In shortStart by defining what you actually want to track, steps, sleep, runs, or all three. Then ignore flashy features you won’t use. A well-reviewed mid-range tracker from a reputable brand is often the smartest buy.
